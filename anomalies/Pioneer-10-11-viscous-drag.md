**Standard Model Status:** Resolved (Thermal Recoil) / Disputed

### 1. The Observation
During their journey into the outer reaches of the solar system, the Pioneer 10 and 11 spacecraft exhibited a constant, unexplained deceleration. A tiny but persistent force was pulling the probes back toward the Sun at a rate of approximately **$8.74 \times 10^{-10} \text{ m/s}^2$**. This effect remained consistent as they traveled between 20 and 70 AU from the Sun.

### 2. The Standard Model Failure
After decades of debate, the "official" explanation attributed the deceleration to **Thermal Recoil Force**. The theory suggests that heat radiating from the spacecraft's electrical systems and plutonium generators pushed back against the craft.

**The Failure:** While thermal modeling accounts for much of the force, the SMP argues this is a "best-fit" excuse for a deeper mechanical reality. The thermal explanation struggles to explain the absolute constancy of the acceleration across two different spacecraft in different trajectories, and it ignores the medium they are traveling through.

* **The Snap Threshold:** As temperatures drop toward the cosmic background, the Matrix transitions into a more rigid, viscous state. 
* **Mechanical Resistance:** The deceleration of $8.74 \times 10^{-10} \text{ m/s}^2$ is the literal "friction" of the spacecraft pushing through the viscuous Syrup. The Pioneer probes were the first instruments sensitive enough to "feel" the thickness of the Matrix.
